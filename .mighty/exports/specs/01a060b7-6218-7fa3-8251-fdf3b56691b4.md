Summary: A source’s change log is a supported read contract for external consumers, not only the sequencer’s private input: a consumer can locate it, tail it by position over the same long-poll protocol shapes use, pin the segments it has not finished, and learn when it has fallen out of retention.
## Guarantees
- **The change log is addressable.** A source’s status names the route its change log is read through and the log’s current position, so a consumer never guesses physical stream keys.
- **Readable by position.** A consumer reads `changes/<n>` pages by `(segment, offset)` with long-poll, receives the same envelopes the sequencer reads (table, key, new row, old row, `lsn`, `txid`, `seq`, `last`), and can follow a rotation to the next segment exactly as the sequencer does.
- **Pinned while unfinished.** A consumer that reports a durable position pins every segment at or after it; the deletion planner never deletes a pinned segment.
- **Bounded by the retain window.** A consumer whose pinned position is older than the retain window is evicted before its segments are deleted, and the consumer is told so on its next read; it re-syncs from state rather than the log replaying history.
- **Observable.** A source lists its external consumers with position and lag; readiness is unaffected by consumers.
## Constraints
- The ingestor stays the only writer of the change log; consumers never append, close, or delete a segment.
- Control envelopes are dropped by type by every reader, never by position; a consumer that fails to do so is non-conformant.
- Delivery to a consumer is at-least-once and transaction-atomic only if the consumer honours `last` and a `(lsn, seq)` highwater, exactly like the sequencer; the engine promises nothing stronger.
- A consumer’s pin can hold a segment only within `ELECTRIC_CIRCUITS_CHANGES_RETAIN_SECS`; beyond it the retain window wins.
## Verifications
- A consumer reads across a rotation: append past the segment budget -> the consumer sees `closed`, drains, steps to exactly the next segment, and observes every commit once.
- A pinned segment survives: consumer position inside segment n, sequencer checkpoint past n -> segment n is not deleted; advancing the pin past n makes it deletable on the next sweep.
- Retain-window eviction: consumer position older than the retain window -> the consumer is evicted first, its segments are deleted, its next read reports gone.
- Status names the log: activate a source -> status carries the change-log route and position; register a consumer -> the source lists it with lag.
- Readiness independence: a stale or evicted consumer never changes `/ready`.
## Rationale
- Mighty’s kernel re-emits every published change into its platform change feed today by polling a transactional outbox in each plugin database; the change log Circuits already keeps per source is the same data, in commit order, durable, with old rows. Making it a supported read contract removes a second database reader and the poll. See the decision below and the reader-facing children.
