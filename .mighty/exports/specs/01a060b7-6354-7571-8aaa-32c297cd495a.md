Summary: The change-log read route hands an external consumer the exact pages the sequencer reads, by position, with the rules the consumer must follow spelled out as a conformance contract.
## Interface
- Engine route `GET /changes/{segment}?offset=<offset>[&live=long-poll]` returns the page from durable streams unchanged and forwards `stream-next-offset`, `stream-up-to-date`, and `stream-closed`; `offset=-1` reads from the segment start. Under circuitsd it is reached as `/sources/{source_id}/changes/{segment}` behind the workload bearer.
- A segment that does not exist answers 404; a segment that was deleted answers 410.
## Guarantees
- Pages are byte-for-byte the change log: envelopes with `type` (`schema.name`), `key`, `value`, `old`, and headers `operation`, `lsn`, `txid`, `seq`, `last`; control envelopes (`type: "__circuits.control"`) are included and the consumer drops them.
- Rotation is followable: after `stream-closed`, a drained read of the closed segment plus the rotation pointer names exactly the next segment.
- Long-poll honours the same deadline as shape reads (`ELECTRIC_LIVE_TIMEOUT_MS`).
## Constraints
- The route never folds, filters, or reorders; it is a positioned window on the log.
- No consumer state is created by reading; pins are explicit (see the consumer-pin spec).
## Verifications
- Round trip: append two transactions -> the route returns both, in order, with `last` on each transaction’s final envelope and `lsn`/`seq` stamps intact.
- Rotation: force a rotation -> the closed segment returns `stream-closed`, its pointer envelope names `changes/<n+1>`, and the successor returns the next commit.
- Held-run conformance fixture: a chunked transaction spread over two pages -> a reference consumer (test helper) reassembles it and emits exactly one transaction.
- Deleted segment -> 410.
